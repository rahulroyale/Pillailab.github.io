About Us

Pillai Lab investigates the molecular and biochemical mechanisms that govern hematopoiesis—the process of blood cell production—in both healthy and disease states.

We combine genome‑wide and transcriptome‑wide approaches with classical hematopoietic assays to uncover how RNA regulation contributes to normal blood formation and how its dysregulation drives disease.

Research

Splicing Factor Mutations and Clonal Hematopoiesis

Most vertebrate genes contain non‑coding introns that must be removed from pre‑mRNA via splicing before translation. Recurrent mutations in key spliceosomal components—including SF3B1, SRSF2, U2AF1, and U1 snRNA—have been identified in clonal hematopoietic disorders such as AML and MDS. We investigate how these mutations disrupt the coordination between splicing and transcription, revealing that mutant splicing factors not only alter alternative splicing but also impair transcriptional kinetics.

Intron Retention in Erythropoiesis and Disease

Some introns are selectively retained, giving rise to intron‑retaining transcripts (IR‑RNAs). Once thought to be splicing errors, these transcripts are now recognized as a regulated layer of gene expression. We study how intron retention is dynamically regulated during normal erythropoiesis and how its dysregulation contributes to MDS pathogenesis.

Transcriptional Regulation in the Bone Marrow Microenvironment

The bone marrow microenvironment consists of diverse cell types that support hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells. We apply functional genomic screens and hematopoietic assays to identify transcription factors that drive niche‑specific gene expression, aiming to reprogram fibroblasts into MPC‑like cells and restore niche function in diseases such as myelofibrosis.
